  • interfaces

    • ▸preludeThe prelude: global settings, basic types and term-building notations replacing the standard library

      This file stands in for Rocq's standard library. It declares the type and function scopes and loads the Ltac, number-notation and firstorder plugins. It also turns on the global settings the rest of the development relies on: primitive projections, universe polymorphism with cumulative inductives, and keyed unification. It sets up the core and typeclass_instances hint databases. It then defines the basic types from scratch: the empty type with abort and dep_abort, unit (which also serves as a trivially satisfied class), bool, the product tprod, the dependent sum tsig, the disjoint union tsum, and ssig, whose elements are points paired with a proof-irrelevant property. Last come the identity function, currying and uncurrying, and a few notations that build terms by running a tactic.

    • ▸notationReserved notation, binders, pairs, and classes for chosen terms and objects

      This file fixes the notation of the whole development: it reserves the symbols for the affine connectives (⊸, ⧟, ⊠, ⊞), the base-logic connectives, equality, apartness and order, the algebraic operations, inclusion, meet and join, and composition, each at its precedence level, so that later files can give them meanings. It defines the binders ∀ and λ, the base-logic arrow → as a non-dependent ∀, the product of types ∗ with tuple notation and the projections π₁ and π₂, and printing notations that show destructured pairs as patterns. It introduces the class The of a term chosen by instance resolution, which later files use for canonical maps such as those out of the empty set and into the one-point set, and the classes BoldZero, BoldOne, Product and Tensor behind the notations 𝟎, 𝟏, × and ⊗ in cat_scope, with the empty type, the unit type and the product of types as their instances on Type.

    • ▸spropThe base-logic connectives on SProp, strict equality, and the classes of strict relations

      This file defines the base-logic connectives on SProp from scratch, since the library uses no standard library: truth and falsity, negation, and, or, implication, if and only if, and the existential quantifier, together with their notations. It adds the class Inhabited, strict equality (seq, a predicate on pairs), and the operations on predicates scomplement and sflip. On binary relations, taken as predicates on pairs (srelation), it defines the classes of subrelation, reflexivity, irreflexivity, symmetry, transitivity and antisymmetry, and their bundles as equivalence and partial equivalence relations, an equivalence being a partial equivalence by coercion. Everything affine in the development is built on these connectives, and logic/srelations.v proves the elementary facts about the relation classes.

    • ▸aprop_kernelThe swappable constructive kernel for affine propositions, enabling the classical conservativity build

      The kernel of the affine propositions: the types of the two side-condition fields of an affine proposition, a pair of a proof component and a refutation component, and one filler per connective building the second field. NotBoth says the proof and refutation cannot both hold; Classicality is trivially true in this constructive kernel, and every filler, for truth, falsity, negation, and, or, tensor, par, implication, of course and the two quantifiers, returns the trivial proof. The file exists to be swapped: the classical conservativity build replaces it with a kernel where the condition is P ∨ N and the fillers come from excluded middle on SProp. Because the rest of the library builds affine propositions only through these fillers (in interfaces/aprop.v), it compiles against both kernels, and every theorem in it is also a theorem of classical mathematics.

    • ▸prop_eqPropositional (Leibniz) equality ≡, registered with the kernel in place of the standard one

      The file defines the library's propositional (Leibniz) equality eq, written x ≡ y, with its constructor eq_refl and its elimination principles into Type, Prop, Set and SProp. The library is built without the standard library, so this equality takes the place of the standard one. The file registers it with the kernel so that Rocq's built-in rewriting and tactics recognise it. Throughout the development, ≡ stands for definitional equality. It is kept separate from the affine equality = of a set, which is an affine proposition and carries a dual.

    • ▸apropAffine propositions Ω, their connectives and quantifiers, and the affine properties of relations

      The file defines Ω, the type of affine propositions: a pair of an affirmation and a refutation in SProp that cannot both hold, together with a classicality field supplied by the kernel. The field is trivial in the constructive kernel and forces decidability in the classical one. It builds the connectives from these pairs: truth and falsity, the additive ∧ and ∨, the tensor ⊠ and the par ⊞, the implication ⊸ carrying its contrapositive, the negation, ⧟, the modalities ! and ?, and the quantifiers ∏ and ∐. On top of these it defines Shulman's classes Decidable, Affirmative and Refutative, De Morgan duals, boolean decision procedures (Dec, IsDec), and the affine properties of relations. Symmetry, transitivity (with ⊠ for the multiplicative form and ∧ for the strong one), antisymmetry, totality and equivalence are each stated as an implication between affine propositions. The laws of these connectives are proved in logic/aprop.v, and the rest of the development is written in this language.

    • ▸relation_notationEquality, order and strict order as affine relations, and the opposite order

      This file declares the two basic relations of the development as typeclasses: equiv, the equality of a type, and le, its order. Each is an affine proposition on pairs, written x = y and x ≤ y, and inequality x ≠ y is the negation of equality. The strict order lt is the complement of the converse order, so x < y is (y ≤ x)ᗮ. The file also defines order_op, a renaming of a type that carries the converse order and the same equality. The opposite orders, and with them the duality between the order notions, are built on this renaming in interfaces/algebra_notation.v and orders/orders.v.

    • ▸setAffine sets, functions between them, products, sums, composition and kinds of maps

      This file defines affine sets. An affine set (set) is a type whose equality is an affine equivalence relation: each instance of it is an affine proposition, so equality and apartness come as one relation. A function X ⇾ Y is a map that preserves equality, x₁ = x₂ ⊸ f x₁ = f x₂, and it therefore also reflects apartness. The functions X ⇾ Y form a set, with equality ∏ x, f x = g x, taken pointwise. Two products are defined on the same carrier: the cartesian product X × Y, whose equality is the additive conjunction ∧, and the tensor product X ⊗ Y, whose equality is the tensor ⊠. Composition is a function on the tensor product (Y ⇾ Z) ⊗ (X ⇾ Y). The file also defines the empty and unit sets, the disjoint sum, and the identity function. It defines the classes for strong sets and for decidable, affirmative and refutative equality, and the injective, weakly surjective, surjective and bijective maps. Everything else in the library is built on these definitions, and theory/set.v develops their theory.

    • ▸catMinimal categories with affine hom-sets, and the category of affine sets

      This file sets up a minimal notion of category in which the objects form a type and the hom-sets are affine sets, so equality of morphisms is an affine equality, carrying apartness as well. It separates the data (cat_t) from the operations (Id and Compose) and from the laws (IsCat), then packages them in the bundled cat. Composition is a function out of the tensor of hom-sets, so it is required to be extensional only in the multiplicative sense. The main instance is 𝐒𝐞𝐭, with affine sets as objects and the function sets of interfaces/set.v as hom-sets, and set_IsCat proves its laws.

    • ▸algebra_notationClasses and notation for algebraic operations; opposite and reinterpreted structures

      The file declares the algebraic operations as classes on affine sets, each with its notation: constants zero, one, top and bottom and a monoid unit; unary inverse and negation; binary semigroup operation, addition, multiplication, meet and join. Every binary operation is a function out of the tensor product X ⊗ X, so that it need only preserve the multiplicative equality; hints let a binary operation that factors through the cartesian product be recognised as strong. It then gives renamings of a set under which one structure is read as another: additive or multiplicative operations, or meets and joins, as a generic semigroup with unit, and the opposite semigroup (semigroup_op), the opposite ring (ring_op) and the opposite order (Order_op), which exchanges meet with join and top with bottom. The algebraic hierarchies of the groups, rings and lattice files build on these classes and use the renamings to obtain each mirrored result from the direct one.

    • ▸bundled_notationOne-field records holding an operation, and hints for resolution to read it back

      The file defines one-field records, each holding a single operation or structure: an order relation (has_le); a monoid unit, zero, one, top or bottom element; a binary operation, addition, multiplication, meet or join; an inversion or negation; or a decision procedure for the equality of a set (has_dec_eq). The constructors of all but the last are coercions. Global hints at priority 4 let instance resolution recover the operation: given a goal such as Plus X, they strip the coercions from X to reach the term underneath and apply the record's projection to it. The module definition_hints adds fallback hints at priority 8 that take the operation from a record found among the hypotheses in context. They cover the unit, zero, one, the binary operation, inversion, addition, negation, multiplication and decidable equality.

    • ▸set_lambdaWires λₛ and set:( … ) notation to the set-internalization tactic

      This file connects the λₛ notation for functions of sets to the set-internalization tactic. It introduces the notation set:( e ), which elaborates an expression by running the internalization procedure on it, so that a term is turned into a set or a function of sets together with its proof of equality preservation. A global hint registers the internalization solver as the default way to discharge the derivation goals that λₛ x, … leaves. Writing λₛ x, … therefore builds the function and proves it preserves equality without further input. The file defines no constants; the notation's syntax lives in interfaces/set_lambda/notation.v and the procedure in tactics/set_internalize.v.

    • ▸common_propsThe standard algebraic laws (associativity, identities, inverses, distributivity) as affine and SProp classes

      This file defines the standard algebraic laws for operations on sets: associativity, commutativity, idempotence of a binary operation, involution and idempotence of a map, absorption, left and right distributivity, identities, absorbing elements, inverses, and distribution and anti-distribution of a map over an operation. Each law is first defined as an affine proposition depending on its operations, in a form that respects equality. Where an operation must occur more than once, it is taken under !. Each affine law is then restated in SProp, in an unfolded form that can serve as a typeclass, and accessors such as associativity, commutativity and left_identity give the equations from an instance. Finally, the _fun canonical structures, built with weaken_apred1, weaken_apred2 and weaken_apred3, make each SProp class respect equality of its operations, so rewriting can replace an operation by an equal one. The algebraic hierarchies built later in the development state their axioms with these classes.

    • ▸abstract_algebraThe algebraic hierarchy: semigroups to integral domains, lattices, and their morphism classes

      This file sets out the algebraic structures of the development as classes on an affine set: the semigroup hierarchy for a generic operation ∙ (semigroups, semilattices, monoids, star semigroups, groups, abelian groups, and the variants whose operation is strong), the lattices from meet and join semilattices to bounded distributive lattices, and the ring hierarchy from near-rgs through rigs, rngs and rings to integral domains, with additive cancellation, 1 ≠ 0 and the two forms of no zero divisors (with ⊞ and with the additive ∨). Lattices and the additive and multiplicative parts of a ring are not separate theories: they are the generic semigroup classes read through the renamings of algebra_notation, so one body of semigroup theory serves all of them. Alongside the structures come the morphism classes: maps preserving a distinguished point, semigroup, monoid and star morphisms, semilattice and lattice morphisms with their order-reversing flip variants, and rg and rig morphisms. The theory of these classes is developed in theory.groups, theory.lattices, theory.additive_groups and theory.rings.

    • ▸pointwisePointwise lifting of operations to function sets, for strong operations or affirmative domains

      The file lifts operations on affine sets to the functions into them. A unary function f : X ⇾ Y lifts by post-composition (pointwise_func) with no further hypothesis. A binary operation f : X ⊗ Y ⇾ Z does not lift in general, because the point d at which g and h are evaluated is needed by both arguments of f while the equality d = d' may be used only once; the class PointwiseOp records that the lift exists, and pointwise_op then gives the function (g, h) ↦ (d ↦ f(g d, h d)). Two sufficient conditions are proved: f is strong, so that it preserves the cartesian equality and the two argument equalities can be proved additively (StrongOp_PointWiseOp), or D has affirmative equality, so that d = d' may be duplicated (AffEq_PointWiseOp). Both are registered for resolution, with the strong case tried first.

    • ▸free_groupsThe interface for free monoids: the extension operation and its universal property

      This file states the interface for free monoids. FromFreeMonoid equips a map i : X ⇾ F with an extension operation sending each f : X ⇾ Y, Y carrying an operation and a unit, to a map F ⇾ Y. FreeMonoid states the universal property: F is a monoid, and for every monoid Y the extension of f is a monoid morphism, agrees with f along i, and is the only monoid morphism that does. The free monoid's monoid structure is available by coercion, and hints let resolution see the extension as a monoid, semigroup and unit-preserving morphism. A free semigroup interface is sketched in a comment but not defined.

    • ▸subsetSubsets: membership, inclusion, subset operations, relations, images, restrictions, subsets as sets

      This file sets up subsets for the affine set theory. Membership x ∊ U is the function element from X ⊗ 𝒫 X to Ω, and it respects equality (equal_element). Inclusion U ⊆ V is ∏ x, x ∊ U ⊸ x ∊ V. The file then defines the basic operations on subsets: the empty and full subsets, singletons, the additive intersection and union, the complement, the multiplicative intersection and union, and the tensor and cartesian pairings of subsets. Relations are subsets of X ⊗ Y, with the diagonal, composition and converse. The file also defines preimage, image, range and the universal image. Next comes the coercion that turns a subset into a set, whose elements are a point together with a proof of its membership, and the injective inclusion from_subset back into X. The classes MapsInto, MapsTo and WeakMapsTo support the corestriction and restriction of functions to subsets. The file ends with the iterated powerset 𝒫². The order-theoretic laws of these operations are proved in the files under orders/.

    • ▸ordersPreorders, posets and their decorations, order maps, lattice orders, filters and ideals

      The file sets up the hierarchy of affine orders. A PreOrder is a reflexive, transitive relation. A WeakPoset adds two things: equal elements are comparable, and x ≤ y ⊠ y ≤ x gives x = y, which is pseudo-antisymmetry with the tensor. A Poset asks for antisymmetry with the additive and. The posets are then decorated as strong, decidable, affirmative, refutative, total or linear, and a three-way comparison (IsTrich) is a linear order with a correct decision procedure. In a weak poset the order and the strict order respect equality (le_fun, lt_fun). Order-preserving, order-reflecting and embedding maps get order-reversing variants through the opposite order. The file also defines meet and join semilattice orders and lattice orders, each characterised by greatest lower and least upper bounds, along with their bounded versions. On subsets it gives up-sets and down-sets, directed subsets, filters and ideals, and the presentations and bases that generate them. Each dual notion is the direct one taken in the opposite order, so it reuses the direct one's theory.

    • ▸subalgebraSubsemigroups, submonoids, subgroups, sub-near-rings and sublattices as closure predicates on subsets

      This file defines substructures as predicates on subsets U : 𝒫 G, each packaging the ambient structure with closure conditions on U: SubSemiGroup (closed under ∙), SubMonoid (also containing the unit), SubStarSemiGroup and SubStarMonoid (closed under the involution), SubGroup, and NormalSubGroup (closed under conjugation y ∙ x ∙ y⁻¹). Binary closure is stated with the tensor, x ∊ U ⊠ y ∊ U ⊸ x ∙ y ∊ U, so each membership is used once and the contrapositive comes with it. The generic notions are renamed for addition and multiplication, with accessors such as sub_add_sg_closed and sub_mul_one_closed in the ring notation, and combined into SubNearRg, SubNearRig, SubNearRng and SubNearRing over the near-ring structures of abstract_algebra. In the same way the meet and join semilattice versions are the semigroup and monoid notions for ⊓ and ⊔, and SubLattice joins the two.

    • ▸bundled_jectionsBundled injections, surjections and bijections between sets, with their categories and the structural bijections

      The file packages functions together with their properties as elements of sets. An injection X ⇾ Y comes with its injectivity, and a surjection or bijection comes with a designated inverse and surjectivity or bijectivity. Each kind forms a set whose equality is pulled back from the function set. Each also forms a category, with identities and a composition that is itself a function of sets. Bijections, written X ≅ Y, can be inverted and can be viewed as injections and as surjections. The tensor product of maps preserves each kind. The symmetries, associators and unitors of the tensor product, and the symmetry and associators of the cartesian product, are registered as canonical bijections.

    • ▸bundled_algebraBundled algebraic structures, from semigroups to commutative rings, with their morphisms and categories

      The file bundles each algebraic structure of the development into a record: a carrier set together with its operations and the proof of its laws. Each record comes with a make_… constructor that finds the operations and laws by resolution, and with a set of morphisms (the structure-preserving functions, compared by their underlying functions). These form the category data for semigroups, monoids, groups, their additive and multiplicative variants, near-rigs, rigs, strong rigs, near-rings, rings and their commutative forms. Coercions arrange the records in a hierarchy, so that a ring is also a rig and a near-ring. Hints let resolution see through these coercions, so a bundled object has the algebraic classes and morphism classes of its unbundled form. The file also makes strong sets into the category 𝐒𝐭𝐫𝐒𝐞𝐭.

    • ▸free_monoidUniversal property of free monoids and free strong monoids, data separated from laws

      The file states the universal property of a free monoid, separating data from laws. FromFreeMonoid gives the data: for a map i from a set X into a monoid Xs, an operation extending each map from X into a monoid M to a monoid morphism from Xs to M. FreeMonoid gives the laws: the extension restricts to the original map along i, and it is the only morphism that does. A parallel pair, FromFreeStrongMonoid and FreeStrongMonoid, states the same property among monoids with a strong operation; there the extension is a function of sets, so it preserves equality and, being affine, apartness as well. Concrete models, such as lists, use these interfaces to show that they are free monoids.

    • ▸group_completionUniversal property of the group completion of an additive monoid

      This file states the universal property of the group completion (Grothendieck group) of an additive monoid. A map i : M ⇾ K from an additive monoid to an additive group comes with an extension operation, FromGroupCompletion, that turns each monoid morphism f : M ⇾ A into a map K ⇾ A, where A is any additive group, abelian or not. FromGroupCompletionProp says that a map g is the unique monoid morphism with g ∘ i = f. GroupCompletion requires this of the extension for every such f, which makes K the free group on M. The file is an interface only: it proves nothing, and concrete models such as the integers over the naturals provide instances.

    • ▸ring_orderCones, and order compatible with addition and multiplication in monoids, groups, rigs and rings

      This file sets out the interfaces for ordered algebra. It first defines the cones of a weak poset with a zero as subsets: the nonzero elements, the nonnegative cone R⁺, the positive cone R₊, the nonpositive cone R⁻ and the negative cone R₋. It then gives the compatibility of order with addition: an ordered additive monoid, where adding z on the left is an order embedding, and an ordered additive group, where adding z on the left preserves the order. On these it builds two classes. A strong linear refutative rig order is a rig with a strong, linear, refutative partial order that is an ordered additive monoid and satisfies b < a ⊠ d < c ⊸ a·d + b·c < a·c + b·d. A strong linear refutative ring order is the ring version, with the same order axioms, an ordered additive group, and closure of the positive elements under products.

    • ▸naturalsInterfaces for the naturals: natural numbers object, initial near-rig, subtraction and order

      This file gives the interfaces for the natural numbers, in two characterisations. The first is Lawvere's natural numbers object: NaturalNumbersObject says that N, with a zero and a Successor, is initial among such sets, with morphisms given by MaybeAlgebra_Morphism. The second, Naturals, is the one the algebraic hierarchy uses. It says that N is a right near-rig initial among additive monoids, not necessarily commutative, with a chosen element 1, and the canonical map sends n to 1 + ⋯ + 1. The bundled record naturals packs this structure. The file also gives an interface, NatSubtract with specification NatSubtractSpec, for comparing two naturals and returning their difference, a substitute for subtraction. Finally, OrderedNaturals adds a strong linear refutative rig order. The theory of naturals and the concrete models build on these classes.

    • ▸integersInterface for the integers as the free group on one generator, and ordered integers

      The file gives the interface for the integers, characterised by a universal property. IntegersToGroup supplies a canonical map from Z into every additive group with a distinguished element, and Integers states that Z is a ring for which this map is the unique additive morphism preserving 1, so that Z is the free group on one generator. The bundled record integers packages a ring with this structure, and OrderedIntegers adds a strong linear refutative ring order, giving the integers as an ordered ring. The theory of the integers and their concrete models build on these classes.

  • interfaces.subset

    • ▸notationSubsets 𝒫 X as functions into Ω, their equality, and comprehension notation

      This file defines the powerset 𝒫 X, the type of subsets of an affine set X, as the functions from X to the affine propositions Ω, following Shulman's *Affine logic for constructive mathematics*. Since an affine proposition pairs proving with refuting, every subset comes with its complement. The canonical set subset_set gives subsets their pointwise equality. subset_comprehension supplies the notation { x | P }, which builds a subset from a predicate that respects equality, through func_make. The files that follow build membership, inclusion and the lattice operations on subsets on top of these definitions.

  • interfaces.set_lambda

    • ▸notationLambda notation for functions of affine sets, with the equality obligation as its own class

      The file sets up the notation λₛ x .. y, t for writing functions between affine sets as lambda terms. Behind it are set_lambda, which packages a raw map as a function of sets through func_make, and SetLambdaDerivation, a separate class name for IsFun that holds the obligation that each such map preserves equality. Because this obligation has its own class, the derivation rules elsewhere in set_lambda can discharge it by following the structure of the term. Two printing-only notations show lambdas that take pairs apart in their destructured form.

  • interfaces.free

    • ▸generatorsSets with generators named by naturals, and evaluation morphisms at an environment

      This file sets up the interface for sets with named generators, which the quoting and normalization tactics use. A Var structure on a set names its generators var 0, var 1, … by the naturals. Var_Morphism says that a function sends each var n to the n-th entry of an environment list Γ, which makes it the evaluation at Γ of terms over the generators. The two are bundled: var_set is a set with named generators, and var_morphism is the set of functions that respect a given environment, with its equality projected from that of functions. The file states definitions only, no theorems; the free structures built on it supply the instances.

    • ▸groupsFree commutative monoids and abelian groups on countably many generators, for the decision tactics

      The file states the interfaces of three free algebraic structures on countably many generators: commutative additive monoids, commutative monoids written multiplicatively, and abelian groups. Each interface has the same three layers. The first is the algebra with named generators and a correct decision procedure for its equality (t and its siblings). The second is the type of evaluation morphisms into a target algebra, which send the n-th generator to the n-th entry of an environment Γ. The third is the free structure itself, an algebra with generators together with an evaluation into every target for every environment. Only the existence half of the universal property is required. That half is what the quoting and normalisation tactics need: they decide an equation in the free structure and carry the verdict to the target along the evaluation. The file builds on the generic notions of has_var and var_morphism_t from interfaces/free/generators.v. Concrete free algebras in implementations/ meet these interfaces.

  • topology.interfaces

    • ▸topologyNeighbourhood topologies: axioms, interior and closure, separation, continuous, reflecting and dense maps, products

      The interface for topological spaces, given by a neighbourhood relation. A Neighborhood on a set X is a function x ⪽ U from X ⊗ 𝒫 X to affine propositions, and Topology states the neighbourhood axioms for it: a neighbourhood contains its point, the relation is upward closed, the full subset is a neighbourhood, binary meets of neighbourhoods are neighbourhoods, and a neighbourhood U is a neighbourhood of the set of points of which U is a neighbourhood. From the relation the file defines interior, closure, open, closed and dense subsets, neighbourhood bases, and the separation axioms Separation_T₀ and Hausdorff. It sets out the classes of maps used throughout the topology files: Continuous, the reflecting class ContinuouslyReflecting, the two together as ContinuouslyInitial, ContinuouslyEmbedding and Dense. It also gives the discrete and indiscrete structures and the two product topologies, the tensor product by a neighbourhood basis and the cartesian product by its universal property. The topological lemmas live in the files under topology/topology, and the uniform and bornological interfaces build on these classes.

    • ▸bornologyBornologies, bornological spaces, presentations, products, and bornological and reflecting maps

      The file sets up bornologies. A bornology on a set X is a set of subsets of X, the bounded sets, and a bornological space is one whose bounded sets form an ideal of subsets that contains every singleton. Presentations and bases of a bornology are the ideal presentations and bases of the order interfaces, read for subsets. The file also defines the trivial bornology, a regularity condition, and the tensor and cartesian product bornologies, each specified by its presentation through rectangles. It then gives the classes of maps: bornological maps (images of bounded sets are bounded), bornology-reflecting maps (preimages of bounded sets are bounded), initial maps and embeddings. Bases and products are developed in the bornology files, and these notions join the uniform structure in the well-containment spaces and in localization.

    • ▸reflection_pairInterface for constructs and reflection pairs: fibres, forward and reflecting classes, cancellation laws

      The file sets up the abstract interface for reflection pairs. A construct is a concrete category over affine sets: each set carries a fibre of candidate structures, some of which are objects, and the forward maps are the functions that respect the structures. The fibre over a set is ordered by comparing structures through the identity, and with equality as two-sided comparison it becomes a poset. Alongside the forward class come a reflecting class, the initial maps (forward and reflecting) and the embeddings (initial and injective). A reflection pair asks for the two classes to form constructs and to satisfy the two cancellation laws. Saturated pairs and cloven pairs refine this, pair morphisms compare two pairs over the same sets, the opposite of a pair swaps the forward and reflecting classes, and binary products are defined in a pair. The theory built on this interface is developed in the files under topology/reflection_pair.

    • ▸uniformUniform spaces: entourages, uniformly continuous and reflecting maps, Cauchy filters, completions

      The file sets up the basic notions of uniform spaces. A uniformity on X is a set of entourages, which are subsets of X ⊗ X. Points x and y are U-close when (x, y) ∊ U. A uniform space is a uniformity that is a filter and satisfies three axioms: reflexivity, symmetry, and splitting (each entourage contains some V ⋄ V). Stronger variants are a splitting with the additive conjunction, separation, and regularity. The uniformity induces a neighbourhood relation. The file then defines the following maps: uniformly continuous and uniformly reflecting maps, uniformly initial maps (both at once), and uniform embeddings. It also defines presentations of the tensor and cartesian product uniformities and of the discrete and indiscrete uniformities, the thickening U.[A] with the relation K ◁ S, and Cauchy filters. Finally it states the universal property of a completion: a separated space Y with a uniformly initial dense map ι from X, such that any uniformly initial dense f : X → Z has a uniformly continuous extension Z → Y with composite ι. The Cauchy completion and the bornological files build on these interfaces.

    • ▸unif_bornUniform spaces with a bornology: well-containment axioms, global and local morphisms, local completions

      The interface for sets carrying both a uniformity and a bornology. It sets up the chassis UnifBornSpace, with no axiom linking the two structures, and then the linking axioms: UniformlyOpenBornology (bounded sets have bounded uniform thickenings), which defines the well-containment uniform spaces WCUnifSpace and their regular form RegularWCUnifSpace, ClosedBornology, and the well-containment relation well_contained. It defines the morphism classes in two flavours. The global ones (UnifBornMorphism, GlobalWCUnifInitial, …) are uniformly continuous and bornological everywhere. The local ones are built on LocalUniformContinuity and LocalUniformReflection, uniform control near bounded sets, and run up to WCUnifMorphism and WCUnifEmbedding. The file closes with the universal property of a local completion, LocalCompletion and LocallyComplete, and with the resolution hints that recover the codomain's structure from a morphism. The underlying structures come from the uniform and bornology interfaces; the theory, the trapped completion and localization are developed in the topology/unif_born files.

  • topology.reflection_pair

    • ▸baseReflection pairs: duality, fibres and pullback, lifting characterisation of initial maps, pair morphisms

      The file develops the basic theory of reflection pairs: the forward and reflecting classes of maps on a kind of structure over sets, with their cancellation laws. Each kind of pair (reflection, cloven, saturated) is closed under the opposite, which exchanges the two classes, so every theorem about forward maps yields its dual about reflecting maps. For a cloven pair it orders the fibre over each set and shows that a map is forward, reflecting or initial exactly when the structure on its domain lies below, above or equal to the structure pulled back along it; pullback is monotone, functorial, and turns every cloven pair into a saturated one. Initial maps are characterised by the lifting property: precomposing with them detects forward maps, also when the tests are restricted to a full subcategory closed under pullback. Finally, morphisms of reflection pairs are shown to preserve objects, initial maps and embeddings, to induce order preserving maps of fibres commuting with pullback, and to compose, with identities.

    • ▸productsProducts in a reflection pair, and products of forward, reflecting, initial maps and embeddings

      The file works with binary products in the forward construct of a reflection pair, given by a PairProduct structure on the cartesian product X × Y. Such a structure is a genuine object (pair_product_obj). The product f × g of two maps is formed with prod_map. If f and g are forward, so is f × g, by the universal property of the product (pair_prod_map_hom). Nothing about products is asked of the reflecting class. Even so, in a saturated pair f × g reflects whenever f and g do (pair_prod_map_rfl): a map h into the domain of f × g is forward as soon as its composite with f × g is, because each component of h then cancels against f or g. Initial maps and embeddings are each split into their parts, so their products follow (pair_prod_map_ini, pair_prod_map_emb). Hints register all four results for instance resolution.

    • ▸completionCompletions and complete objects for a reflection pair over topological spaces

      The theory of completion for a reflection pair C that a pair morphism carries to topological spaces (𝐀𝐓𝐨𝐩), so that density and the Hausdorff property of C-objects are read in the underlying topology. A completion of X (PairCompletion) is a dense initial map ι into a Hausdorff object through which every dense initial test out of X factors by a forward map. A complete object (CompleteObj) is one whose identity is a completion. The file shows that completions are complete, that every completion is isomorphic to a canonical one (CanonicalCompletion), and that the universal property extends from initial tests to dense reflecting tests (pair_completion_reflect, complete_obj_inverse). It builds the extension operator canonical_completion_ext, proves that the canonical completion is an idempotent monad whose functor inverts dense initial tests, shows that preserving initial maps is equivalent to a descent law for initiality along dense maps (map_ini_descent_iff), and proves that products of completions are completions and products of complete objects are complete.

  • logic

    • ▸srelationsBase-logic relations: converse, complement, equality, implication, and congruence of the connectives

      The file proves the basic facts about relations of the base logic, the predicates on pairs valued in SProp that interfaces/sprop.v introduces, and makes them available to instance resolution. Taking the converse (sflip) preserves reflexivity, irreflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, antisymmetry, subrelations, partial equivalences and equivalences. Taking the complement (scomplement) turns irreflexive relations into reflexive ones and reflexive relations into irreflexive ones, preserves symmetry, and reverses inclusion. Strict equality is an equivalence relation contained in every reflexive relation. Implication is a reflexive and transitive relation, antisymmetric with respect to logical equivalence, and logical equivalence is an equivalence relation. The rest are congruence lemmas: a transitive relation, or a partial equivalence relation, respects related arguments (sTransitive_rel_proper_impl, sPER_rel_proper_iff); implication, equivalence, negation, disjunction and conjunction respect implication and equivalence of their arguments; and the four tactics do the same for the quantifiers. All of these are registered in the proper hint database, which the rewriting system of tactics/ uses whenever a rewrite reaches into a base-logic statement.

    • ▸apropLaws of the affine connectives: duals, tautologies, congruences, and decidable, affirmative, refutative propositions

      The algebra of affine propositions on which the rest of the development rewrites. The file registers De Morgan duals for every connective and quantifier, so that the negation of a statement is found in normal form, and it states contraposition in that form. It proves the laws of the connectives as equivalences: associativity, commutativity, units, absorption and distribution for ∧, ∨, ⊠ and ⊞; currying (P ⊠ Q ⊸ R) ⧟ (P ⊸ Q ⊸ R); the quantifier rules, instantiation and the Frobenius laws; and the relations between !P, ?P, P ⊠ P and P ⊞ P. It makes ⊸ a preorder and ⧟ an equivalence, and registers the congruence lemmas that let the rewriting tactics work under every connective. Finally it shows that the classes Decidable, Affirmative and Refutative are closed under the connectives, and that tensor and additive and, and par and additive or, coincide when one side is decidable.

    • ▸relationsGeneral laws of affine relations: strong and pseudo variants, converse, complement, congruence

      This file collects the general facts about Ω-valued relations that the rest of the development relies on. It relates the strong and weak variants of the order-theoretic properties: StronglyTransitive_Transitive, Antisymmetric_PseudoAntisymmetric and Total_PseudoTotal weaken the additive forms to the multiplicative ones, and dec_trans_strong, dec_pseudo_antisym and dec_pseudo_total recover the additive forms for decidable relations. It shows that these properties pass to the converse (flip) and, where they apply, to the complement, and it proves the congruence lemmas Transitive_rel_proper_aimpl and PER_rel_proper_aiff that let rewriting move along a transitive relation or a partial equivalence. Finally it compares affine relations with relations in the base logic: a strict relation affirmed pointwise by of course inherits reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity and equivalence, and the converse also holds. The file also gives the trivial equivalence trivial_Equivalence and the affirmed strict equality leq_equiv.

    • ▸refutativeExcluded middle as a par, case analysis for refutative goals, and affirmative decidability

      This file collects the classical reasoning that is valid when the conclusion is refutative. It proves the affine law of excluded middle P ⊞ Pᗮ, which holds as a par and not as a disjunction, and the related fact ?(P ∨ Pᗮ). Since a refutative Q follows from why not P whenever P ⊸ Q (refutative_by_why_not), these give elimination rules for refutative goals: case analysis on a par (refutative_apar_elim), on P or its negation (refutative_by_LEM, refutative_by_cases), and proof by contradiction (refutative_by_contradiction). The file then defines Affirmative_Decidability, which says that P ∨ Pᗮ is affirmative. When P has this property, the case split may use a base-logic function from P ∨ Pᗮ to the conclusion (refutative_by_aff_cases). Affirmative and refutative propositions, !P and ?P are all affirmatively decidable, and the property is preserved by negation, ∧, ∨, ⊠ and ⊞; these facts are registered as instances.

    • ▸decBoolean decision functions for affine relations, and proving their instances by computation

      The file collects lemmas for using a relation R together with a boolean decision function that is correct for it, in the sense of IsDec: the boolean dec R x is true when R x holds and false when its negation does. dec_spec_by_iff transports this correctness along an equivalence R x ⧟ P, dec_spec_dual states the false branch by a De Morgan dual of R x instead of its negation, and dec_spec_andb combines two decisions by boolean conjunction, giving R₁ x₁ ∧ R₂ x₂ on true and R₁ x₁ᗮ ∨ R₂ x₂ᗮ on false. The main result, dec_spec_true, is the equivalence R x ⧟ (dec R x = true), and the tactic decide_relation uses it to prove closed instances of a decidable relation by computing the boolean.

      The file supplies tauto and intuition for the base logic SProp.     • ▸set_internalizeThe set_internalize tactic: turning internal-language terms into functions of sets

      This file implements set_internalize, a tactic that takes a term written in the internal language of affine sets and recovers the function of sets it denotes. Coercions from sets to types and from functions to maps let a term like λ x y, f y x be written directly. The tactic reads that term's syntax and builds the corresponding morphism by interpreting each typing judgment as a function out of its context. The context records how each variable may be used.     • ▸setAffine sets: Ω and SProp as sets, the ! comonad, product structure maps, currying, bijections

      This file develops the basic theory of affine sets and their functions. Ω becomes a set with ⧟ as its equality, and SProp a set with ↔ as its equality.       This file proves generic lemmas about binary operations on affine sets.     • ▸baseBasic topology: classes of maps under composition, separation, discrete and indiscrete spaces

      The first results about topological spaces given by a neighbourhood relation. The classes of maps (continuous, reflecting, initial, embedding, dense) become predicates that respect equality of maps, and each is closed under identity and composition. On separation: a Hausdorff space is T₀, and in a Hausdorff space two points are equal exactly when all their neighbourhoods meet.       This file develops the basic algebra of the interior and closure operators that a topology determines from its neighbourhood relation. The interior is contained in its argument, is monotone and idempotent, and preserves the full subset and the empty subset. It also sends the multiplicative intersection of two interiors into the interior of their meet. As a consequence, interiors are open, and an open subset is a neighbourhood of each of its points. The closure, defined as the complement of the interior of the complement, has the dual properties, which follow by complementation: it contains its argument, is idempotent, and sends a join into the multiplicative union of the closures. A subset is open exactly when its complement is closed, and conversely. The file ends with the neighbourhood characterisation of the closure, that x is in the closure of U if and only if every neighbourhood of x meets U, and its consequences for dense subsets. These results are used by the files on continuity, uniform spaces and completions.

A map f is uniformly continuous near bounded sets exactly when f ∘ ε is uniformly continuous (localized_uc, localized_uc_conv). For f dense into a well-containment space, f is locally uniformly reflecting exactly when η ∘ f is uniformly reflecting (localized_ur, localized_ur_conv). The localization preserves separation and well-containment, collapses to X under a trivial bornology, and is idempotent. The file then lifts local maps to global maps between localizations, and from these derives the dense descent results of unif_born/local_maps.v for the local classes.

    • ▸trapped_cauchyThe trapped completion: Cauchy filters with a bounded member, the canonical well-containment completion

      The file builds the trapped completion 𝒯 X of a uniform space with a bornology: the Cauchy filters having a bounded member, as a subset of the Cauchy completion 𝒞 X with the uniformity pulled back along the inclusion β X, and the unit τ X : X ⇾ 𝒯 X, through which the unit of 𝒞 X factors. The unit is uniformly initial and dense, and 𝒯 X is separated. The entourages T X U lifted from X give a basis of its uniformity. The bornology of 𝒯 X is generated by the closures of the images of bounded sets under τ X. It is closed, it is well contained when X is, and τ X is bornologically initial when the bornology of X is closed or well contained. Bornological maps extend to maps 𝒯₁ f, so 𝒯 is a functor natural in the unit. Along a dense reflecting map into a well-containment space, the reflection of the Cauchy completion lands in 𝒯 X. Together these make (𝒯, τ, 𝒯₁, the reflection) the canonical completion of well-containment uniform spaces with global maps, and that completion preserves initial maps.

    • ▸trapped_wcThe trapped completion in intrinsic local terms: functor action, reflection, local completion

      This file states the trapped completion 𝒯 X of a well-containment uniform space in intrinsic terms, by passing the constructions of the file on trapped Cauchy filters through the localization ℒ once. A trapped Cauchy filter on X is also Cauchy for the localized uniformity. This gives trapped_localize : 𝒯 X ⇾ 𝒯 ℒ X, the inverse of 𝒯₁ (ε X), which is a bijection and an embedding of well-containment spaces. With it the file defines the functor action wc_trapped_map on locally uniformly continuous morphisms and the reflection wc_trapped_reflect of a dense, locally reflecting map. Both take hypotheses and give results in the local classes, so code that uses them need not mention ℒ. The file ends with trapped_local_completion: the unit τ X is a local completion.     • ▸local_completionLocal completions of well-containment uniform spaces, through the reflection-pair completion theory

      This file instantiates the abstract completion theory of reflection pairs at well-containment uniform spaces (𝐖𝐂𝐔𝐧𝐢𝐟), with the trapped completion 𝒯 as the canonical completion. It first relates the concrete notions to the pair-level ones in both directions: a local completion is a pair completion (wcunif_pair_completion) and conversely (wcunif_completion), and likewise for locally complete spaces. It then shows that 𝒯 X with its unit τ, the induced maps wc_trapped_map and the reflections wc_trapped_reflect form a canonical completion that preserves initial maps. From this the general theorems of reflection_pair/completion.v carry over: completions are locally complete, the universal property extends to dense reflecting maps, a separated space with a dense locally complete subspace is locally complete, a retraction of τ X gives local completeness, and a dense reflecting map has a locally uniformly continuous extension wc_cont_ext. Last, a complete uniform space with a well-containment bornology is locally complete.

    • ▸open_immersion_completeAn open subset of a locally complete well-containment space is locally complete

      This file proves that an open immersion inherits local completeness. X is a locally complete well-containment uniform space and S an open subset of it. A trapped Cauchy filter on 𝒪 S is pushed forward along the inclusion j S, and its limit in X is taken through the inverse of the bijective trapped unit τ X. This limit lies in S: the bounded member K of the filter has j⁎ K uniformly below S, so the closure of j⁎ K is still contained in S, and the limit lies in that closure (unif_below_closure, to_trapped_eq_closure). Corestricted to S, the limit map is a continuous retraction of τ (𝒪 S), and to_trapped_retract_complete then gives open_immersion_locally_complete. The file claims no intrinsic completeness of the localization of 𝒪 S, only that trapped filters converge in it.

    • ▸subspacePullbacks and subspaces of uniform spaces with bornologies, and well-containment spaces

      This file gives the initial, or pullback, structure for uniform spaces with bornologies. A uniform space with a bornology has no axiom linking its two components, so the initial structure along f : X ⇾ Y is the pair made of the pullback uniformity and the pullback bornology, and f is initial for it. Pullback therefore serves as the cleavage of the reflection pair 𝐔𝐧𝐢𝐟𝐁𝐨𝐫𝐧 (unifborn_cloven_pair), and initial maps are characterised by testing against all maps from other uniform spaces with bornologies (unif_born_initial_alt). The well-containment axiom survives pullback (pullback_wc_unif_space), so the same test can be restricted to well-containment spaces (wcunif_initial_alt). Taking f to be a subset inclusion gives the subspace structure, and the inclusion is an embedding (from_subset_unif_born_emb). The file builds on the uniform and bornological subspace files.

    • ▸bounded_subspaceBounded subspaces: unchanged by localization; local uniform continuity versus uniform continuity on bounded sets

      This file treats a bounded set K of a space with a uniformity and a bornology as a subspace, with the subspace uniformity and bornology. That bornology is trivial, since every subset of a bounded set is bounded. So the localization does not change the subspace, and the inclusion of K into ℒ X is a uniformly continuous embedding (bounded_subspace_localized_uc, bounded_subspace_localized_emb). As a consequence, a locally uniformly continuous map is uniformly continuous on each bounded subspace (locally_uc_bounded_restriction). Dually, a locally uniformly reflecting map is uniformly reflecting on each bounded restriction (locally_ur_bounded_corestriction, locally_ur_bounded_restriction). For a regular well-containment space the converses hold (bounded_restrictions_local_uc, bounded_restrictions_local_ur). Their proofs find the needed entourages with the thickening axiom and both regularities.

  • topology.uniform

    • ▸baseUniform spaces: entourage algebra, thickenings, uniform topology, uniform maps, the reflection pair

      The basic theory of uniform spaces. It equips a uniformity with its algebraic structure (a sublattice and sub-star-semigroup of relations), derives the splitting and symmetry consequences of the axioms and the composition laws for closeness, and develops thickenings U[A] of subsets: monotonicity, composition, singletons, and the swap law for a thickening apart from a set. The uniform topology is built from the entourages, with interior, closure and density described by entourages; separated uniform spaces are shown Hausdorff, and regular ones strong. For maps it characterises uniform continuity and reflection by preimages under f × f, introduces the pullback of entourages ufm_preimage, proves closure under identity, composition and the cancellation laws, and assembles uniform spaces into the reflection pair 𝐔𝐧𝐢𝐟 with a morphism to topological spaces. It ends with thickening and closure estimates along dense and uniformly reflecting maps, used by the completion and trapped-completion files.

    • ▸uniformly_belowThe uniformly-below relation K ◁ S on subsets of a uniform space

      The file develops the basic theory of the uniformly-below relation of pointfree topology on the subsets of a uniform space: K ◁ S holds when some thickening U[K] lies in S, that is, when S is a uniform neighbourhood of K (uniformly_below). The relation is contained in inclusion and is transitive. It is monotone, shrinking on the left and growing on the right (unif_below_proper_aimpl). The empty subset is uniformly below every subset, and every subset is uniformly below the whole set. The relation is closed under joins on the left and meets on the right, with the two entourages that the two hypotheses provide combined by their meet (unif_below_join, unif_below_meet). It interpolates through a thickening (unif_below_interpolate), and it may be strengthened to the closure on the left and to the interior on the right; in particular K ◁ S gives K ⊆ interior S, and every point of the interior has its singleton uniformly below S. Supporting monotonicity lemmas for thickening (thicken_le, thicken_le_r, thicken_join_le) and hints that register the inclusion, the transitivity and the monotonicity with the rewriting system complete the file.

    • ▸basisPresented uniformities and bases; discrete and indiscrete uniformities; trivial uniformly continuous maps

      This file works with uniformities given by a presentation or a basis. It builds the uniformity presented by a family of relations. It gives conditions on the generators (each contains the diagonal, has an inverse below it and a square below it) under which the result is a pre-uniform space or a uniform space (presented_pre_uniform_space, presented_uniform_space). Conversely, it shows that a basis of a uniform space is inhabited, down-directed, symmetric and splits. It restates separation, uniform continuity and uniform reflection (ufm_cont_by_basis, ufm_refl_by_basis), interior, closure and density (uniform_basis_interior, uniform_basis_closure, uniform_basis_Dense_iff) with quantifiers over a basis instead of over all entourages. The discrete and indiscrete uniformities are defined as presented uniformities: every map out of a discrete space is uniformly continuous, and so is every map into an indiscrete one. Every uniform structure on 𝟏 is indiscrete, and maps out of 𝟎 and constant maps are uniformly continuous.

    • ▸productCartesian and tensor product uniformities, their structure maps, and dense-extension criteria

      The file builds the cartesian and tensor product uniformities from their presentations by basic entourages, and shows that each makes the product a uniform space. Basic entourages of X × Y combine U-closeness of first coordinates and V-closeness of second coordinates additively. Those of X ⊗ Y combine them by the tensor. For the cartesian product it proves that the projections are uniformly continuous and that a map into the product is uniformly continuous when both of its components are. It also shows that the induced topology is the cartesian product topology and that a product of separated spaces is separated. For the tensor product it shows that the induced topology is the tensor product topology, and that the unitors, the symmetry and the associators are uniform embeddings. On both products, maps of factors that are uniformly continuous, reflecting, initial or embeddings give product maps of the same kind. The last section, on relations in the tensor square, describes the interior and closure of an entourage by thickening and derives the dense-extension criteria ufm_dense_reflecting and ufm_dense_continuous.

    • ▸cylinderCylinders π₁* K ⊓ W in X ⊗ X, their thickenings, and interiors

      The file studies cylinders in X ⊗ X: subsets π₁* K ⊓ W, the pairs whose first point lies in K and which are W-close. It shows that the image of an entourage under the second projection is the whole space (ufm_proj2_image_entourage), that the cylinder over a thickening U.[K] is the inverse entourage composed with the cylinder over K (proj1_preimage_thicken), and that composing a cylinder with an entourage leaves it unchanged (ufm_compose_proj1_preimage). From these, a symmetric W with W ∙ W ∙ W ≤ U ⊓ V carries π₁* K ⊓ W, thickened by W on both sides, into π₁* U.[K] ⊓ V (cylinder_thicken). So every such cylinder target contains a smaller cylinder in its interior for the product uniformity (cylinder_sub_interior). This is the core that the dense-descent theorems for well-containment uniform spaces and the reflecting universal property of the localization reduce to; the subset-level projection algebra it builds on is in orders/subset_images.v.

    • ▸inducedA separated uniform space induced on a type by a basis of relations

      Builds a separated uniform space from a type with an affine equality and a family of relations indexed by an inhabited Λ. The relations must be reflexive, symmetric up to a change of index, split under composition, form a downward-directed family, be compatible with equality, and determine the equality: x = y if and only if x and y are related by every basic relation. The equality is then an equivalence, so the type is a set; each basic relation is a subset of X ⊗ X; and the uniformity they present makes X a separated uniform space. The proofs apply the general lemmas on presented filters and uniformities from uniform/basis.v and orders/filters.v.

    • ▸cauchy_completionThe Cauchy completion: Cauchy filters, their uniformity, the unit η, and its universal property

      The file builds the Cauchy completion 𝒞 X of a uniform space X. Its points are the Cauchy filters on X, bundled as cauchy_filter. Two Cauchy filters are equal (cauchy_equiv) when they have U-close members for every entourage U. An entourage U of X lifts to the relation cauchy_basis, which holds of F and G when for every V they have members A and B with A ⊗ B ⊆ V⁻¹ ∙ U ∙ V. These relations present a separated uniformity on 𝒞 X (cauchy_uniformity, cauchy_filter_space), and the lifted entourages cauchy_entourage come with laws for inverses, composition and members. The unit to_cauchy sends a point to its principal filter; it is uniformly initial, dense, and an embedding when X is separated. A uniformly continuous f extends to cauchy_map, which commutes with η and preserves composition and identities. From a dense, uniformly reflecting f : X ⇾ Y, cauchy_reflect builds a map Y ⇾ 𝒞 X with cauchy_reflect f ∘ f = η X. This gives the universal property cauchy_completion. The trapped completion in topology.unif_born.trapped_cauchy reuses several of these lemmas.

    • ▸completionUniform completions as an instance of the reflection-pair completion theory

      This file specialises the completion theory of reflection pairs to uniform spaces under the label 𝐔𝐧𝐢𝐟. It first shows that a uniform completion in the sense of Completion is a pair completion, through unif_pair_completion and its converse unif_completion, so that the abstract theorems apply whenever a concrete completion is in context. The Cauchy filters with η, 𝒞 f and the reflect maps form the canonical completion (unif_canonical_completor, unif_canonical_completion), and the file shows that this completion preserves initial maps. From these come the uniform versions of the main results: completions are complete, the space of Cauchy filters 𝒞 X is complete, and the universal property extends to dense uniformly reflecting maps (completion_reflect) and to uniformly continuous maps into a complete space (ufm_cont_ext). Several criteria for completeness follow, and the file closes by showing that cartesian products of completions are completions and products of complete spaces are complete.

    • ▸quotientThe quotient uniformity along a weakly surjective map, and its universal property

      This file defines the quotient uniformity that a map f : X ⇾ Y carries from a uniformity on X to Y. Its presentation consists of the images under f ⊗ f of the entourages of X (entourage_pushforward, quotient_uniformity), and a basis of the uniformity on X gives a basis of the quotient (quotient_uniformity_basis_correct). Suppose X is a uniform space, f is weakly surjective, and every entourage contains the preimage of the diagonal of Y. Then Y is a uniform space (quotient_uniform_space) and f is uniformly continuous (quotient_map_ufm_cont). The quotient uniformity is also final: a map g out of Y is uniformly continuous whenever g ∘ f is (quotient_uniformity_final). The file builds on the presented uniformities of uniform/basis.v.

    • ▸subspacePulled-back uniformities, uniformly initial maps, and uniform subspaces

      The file pulls a uniformity back along a function f : X ⇾ Y: the entourages of X are the relations containing the preimage under f × f of an entourage of Y. This is presented by the preimages of the entourages, a basis of Y pulls back to a basis, and when Y is a uniform space so is X (pullback_uniform_space), with f uniformly initial, and a uniform embedding when f is injective. The universal property (pullback_uniformity_initial) makes the construction a cleavage of the reflection pair of uniform spaces (unif_cloven_pair), which gives the characterisations of uniformly initial maps by lifting (uniformly_initial_alt, uniformly_initial_alt2). Subspaces are the case of the inclusion of a subset: the inclusion is a uniform embedding, a subspace of a separated space is separated, and a dense map restricted over an open subset stays dense (dense_open_restrict).

    • ▸finalThe final uniformity of a family of maps, and the largest uniformity inside a filter

      The file builds the final uniformity on a set Y for a family of maps f i : X i ⇾ Y out of uniform spaces. A filter L of relations on Y need not be reflexive, symmetric or closed under splitting. uniformity_of_filter takes the largest uniformity below L: the upward closure of the pre-uniformities contained in L. Meets work because preuniformity_join combines two pre-uniformities into one, and uniformity_of_filter_space proves the result is a uniform space. The relations whose preimage under every ⟨f i, f i⟩ is an entourage form the filter final_semiuniformity, and final_uniformity is the uniformity it generates. Every f i is uniformly continuous for it (sink_map_ufm_cont). By final_uniformity_final, a map g out of Y is uniformly continuous when every g ∘ f i is; the proof goes through pullback_uniformity.

    • ▸factorizationDense/closed-embedding factorization of uniform maps, diagonal property, and the essential uniqueness of completions

      The file sets up the (dense, closed embedding) factorization of uniformly continuous maps. Every uniformly continuous f factors as a dense uniformly continuous corestriction onto the closure of its range (image_closure, image_corestrict) followed by the inclusion of that closed subspace (image_factor). The diagonal property holds for a dense map against the inclusion of a closed subspace (diagonal, diagonal_unique), and any two such factorizations are related by a uniform isomorphism (ess_iso_bijective); in both, uniqueness rests on the inclusion being injective, with no separation of the ambient space assumed. The same argument, run with the comparison maps of the completion and cont_dense_epi, shows that any two completions of a space are uniformly isomorphic, compatibly with their embeddings and uniquely (firm_iso_bijective, firm_iso_unique). The file ends with a cancellation law: if g ∘ f is a uniform embedding with closed range and f is dense, then g has closed range and, when its domain is separated, is a uniform embedding (cancel_closed_range, cancel_embedding).

  • topology.bornology

    • ▸baseBornological maps, their composition and cancellation, and the bornological reflection pair

      This file covers the basic theory of maps between bornological spaces. A bornology is a sublattice and a bounded join-subsemilattice of the subsets. The bornological, bornology-reflecting, initial and embedding classes of maps respect equality of functions. Each class contains the identity and is closed under composition, and two cancellation laws hold: bornological_factor and born_refl_factor. Together these make the label 𝐁𝐨𝐫𝐧 a reflection pair (born_rfl_pair). The general theory of reflection pairs then shows that inverting a bijection exchanges the forward and reflecting classes. The file ends with the map born_pt, which sends a point to its bounded singleton, and a pointwise form of bornological regularity, bornology_regularity_alt.

    • ▸basisBornologies presented by bases, basis criteria for maps, the trivial bornology

      This file builds bornologies from bases and gives criteria stated in terms of bases. presented_bornology turns any family of subsets into the bornology it generates downward. presented_bornological_space shows that this bornology satisfies the axioms of a bornological space when the family is order-preserving on an up-directed index set and covers every point. Conversely, a basis of a bornological space covers the points, has an inhabited index set and is up-directed. born_by_basis and bornology_refl_by_basis check that a map is bornological, or bornology-reflecting, by checking basis sets only. The file then treats the trivial bornology, in which every subset is bounded, and shows that maps out of the empty set, maps into the one-point set and constant maps are bornological; every bornology on the one-point set is trivial.

    • ▸productTensor and cartesian product bornologies, their bases, and the bornological structure maps

      The file equips the tensor product X ⊗ Y and the cartesian product X × Y of two bornological spaces with their canonical product bornologies, tensor_product_bornology and cartesian_product_bornology, each the bornology presented by the rectangles of bounded sets, and shows that bases of the factors give bases of the products. For the cartesian product it proves that the projections are bornological and that a map into X × Y is bornological as soon as its two components are (cartesian_product_bornology_initial), so pairing and the product of maps preserve bornological, reflecting and initial maps. For the tensor product it proves the same for the tensor of maps, and that the unitors, the symmetry and the associators are bornologically initial; the tensor projections and the comparison X ⊗ Y ⇾ X × Y are then bornological, being composites of these. It builds on the presentations and basis criteria of the bornology base and basis files.

    • ▸subspaceInitial (pullback) bornologies along a map, subspace bornologies, and bornologies as a cloven reflection pair

      The file builds the initial bornology along a map f : X ⇾ Y: the subsets of X whose image under f is bounded. It proves that this is a bornology, that f is bornological and bornologically initial for it, and installs it as the cleavage of the reflection pair of bornological spaces, which makes that pair cloven and saturated (born_cloven_pair). From the general theory of reflection pairs it obtains the characterisations of initial maps by cancellation against arbitrary maps g (bornology_initial_alt, bornology_initial_alt2). Subspaces are the case of a subset's inclusion: that inclusion is a bornological embedding (from_subset_born_emb), and a bounded subset carries the trivial subspace bornology (bounded_pullback_bornology_trivial). This parallels the subspace construction for uniform spaces.

  • counterexamples

    • ▸cartesian_productCounterexample: topological spaces do not all have cartesian products

      This file shows that the existence of cartesian products of arbitrary topological spaces is contradictory. The counterexample is a three-point strong set X with points x, x′ and y. The equality x = x′ is the affine proposition zero_zero, which has no proof and no refutation. The topology on X, X_topology, makes x and y isolated and puts y in every neighbourhood of x′. If a topology on X × X had the universal property CartesianProductTopology, it would affirm that the singleton M of (x, x) is a neighbourhood of (x, x). Through the comparison map from the tensor product, it would also refute that M is a neighbourhood of (x′, x′). Strong extensionality of the neighbourhood map (apos_aneg_apart) then gives (x, x) ≠ (x′, x′), and that inequality is false. The main results are product_UP_False and products_imply_False. Together they explain why the product topology is stated as a certificate that a particular product exists, and not as a construction.
